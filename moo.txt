1. PARETO-DOMINANCE-BASED (same family as NSGA-II/III)
=====================================================================
Extended 2026-08-27 with a dedicated search for the rest of this
family (historical lineage + any 2025/2026 successors), beyond the
SPEA2/PESA-II pair already logged above. Listed roughly in
chronological/lineage order, since each one is a direct reaction to
the previous one's weakness -- useful for framing a "why NSGA-II"
paragraph in the paper.

- VEGA (Vector Evaluated Genetic Algorithm), 1985
  The original: pioneered using an EA for multi-objective problems by
  splitting the population into sub-groups, each selected on a single
  objective. Suffered from poor diversity and biased ("speciated")
  convergence toward single-objective extremes rather than a spread
  Pareto front -- the problem every algorithm below was invented to
  fix.
  -> Historical baseline only; no reuse value for this project, not a
     candidate to implement.

- MOGA (Multi-Objective Genetic Algorithm), 1993
  Introduced Pareto-rank-based fitness assignment plus fitness sharing
  for diversity, replacing VEGA's per-objective sub-population split.
  -> Historical; superseded by every later entry on this list.

- NPGA (Niched Pareto Genetic Algorithm), Horn et al. 1993
  Early diversity-preservation approach: Pareto-dominance tournament
  selection combined with fitness sharing in a niche.
  -> Historical; same role as MOGA above.

- SPEA (Strength Pareto EA), 1999
  Predecessor to SPEA2: strength-based fitness (an individual's
  fitness derived from how many others it dominates) plus an external
  archive of non-dominated solutions, but without SPEA2's k-th-nearest-
  neighbor density estimator.
  -> Superseded by SPEA2 (already logged above); listed here only for
     lineage completeness.

- PAES (Pareto Archived Evolution Strategy), 1999
  Distinct design point from the GA-population-based algorithms above:
  a (1+1) evolution strategy -- no crossover, a single parent/child
  pair per iteration -- using dominance-relation-based local search and
  an archive with adaptive grid-based diversity maintenance instead of
  crowding/sharing.
  A 2025 paper gives the first runtime analysis of "PAES-25", an
  enhanced PAES variant, suggesting the algorithm is still an active
  research subject, not purely historical.
  -> Different mechanism (single-solution ES, not population GA) from
     everything else in this project's candidate lists -- would need
     its own chromosome-mutation/local-search framing rather than
     slotting into the existing optimise_block_* pattern. Not
     prioritized, but worth naming as evidence NSGA-II's population-
     based approach was one design choice among several viable ones.

- PESA (Pareto Envelope-based Selection Algorithm), 2000, and
  PESA-II, 2001
  PESA introduced the hyper-grid ("hyperbox") archive for diversity;
  PESA-II is the same idea with selection performed at the hyperbox
  level rather than the individual level (fewer, cheaper comparisons).
  PESA-II was already logged above; PESA itself added here for
  lineage completeness only.
  -> Not prioritized (no reuse of existing project code, unlike
     SMS-EMOA/MOSA).

- SPEA2 (Strength Pareto EA 2), 2001 -- carried over from the prior
  entry (unchanged from the first version of this file):
  Fitness from a "strength" count (how many solutions each individual
  dominates) plus a k-th-nearest-neighbor density estimator, with a
  fixed-size external archive.
  A 2025 theoretical paper (IJCAI 2025, "SPEA2 Beats NSGA-II") proved
  SPEA2 achieves polynomial-time optimal Pareto-front approximation
  guarantees, while NSGA-II's best proven guarantee is only ~2x.
  A 2026 follow-up, SPEA2+, improves its density estimator with
  provable runtime guarantees.
  -> Already on logs.txt's candidate list (#4, SPEA2) as a baseline
     comparison against NSGA-II's crowding distance; this search adds
     a stronger, more current citation for it than a plain baseline
     description would have had.

- Weak Constraint-Pareto Dominance algorithm, Nov 2025 (MDPI
  Mathematics)
  A 2025-vintage successor in this lineage aimed at *constrained*
  multi-objective problems: a "weak constraint-Pareto dominance"
  relation that integrates feasibility with objective performance,
  plus an angle-distance-based diversity maintenance strategy.
  -> Not directly applicable today -- this project's per-block
     objectives (fidelity, depth, cost) are unconstrained in the
     formal sense (no hard feasibility region to violate), so the
     "weak constraint" mechanism has nothing to attach to. Worth
     revisiting only if a future objective becomes a hard constraint
     rather than a soft objective (e.g. "fidelity >= 0.999" as a
     feasibility cutoff instead of a maximized objective).

2. INDICATOR-BASED
=====================================================================
- SMS-EMOA (S-Metric Selection EMOA)
  Selection driven directly by hypervolume contribution; removes
  whichever individual would shrink the front's hypervolume least.
  High-quality, well-distributed fronts but computationally expensive
  (hypervolume calculation scales badly with objective count).
  -> Already implemented in this project (see logs.txt, "RESEARCH
     ANGLE CHOSEN AND IMPLEMENTED", 2026-08-25); confirmed by this
     search as one of the standard indicator-based choices, not a
     niche pick.

- IBEA (Indicator-Based EA)
  Generalizes SMS-EMOA's idea to any binary quality indicator
  (hypervolume, epsilon, etc.), not just hypervolume.
  Comparative literature: IBEA wins hypervolume on 6/10 problems with
  3+ objectives in one cited study -- often competitive with or better
  than NSGA-II/MOEA/D on many-objective problems.
  -> Already on logs.txt's candidate list (#2, reuses compute_epsilon).
     Not yet implemented in this codebase.

=====================================================================
3. DECOMPOSITION-BASED
=====================================================================
- MOEA/D (Decomposition-based MOEA)
  Splits the multi-objective problem into scalar subproblems via
  weight vectors (Tchebycheff / weighted-sum / PBI), solved
  cooperatively with neighborhood information sharing.
  Widely available pre-built (e.g. in pymoo, already a project
  dependency per requirements notes).
  -> Already on logs.txt's candidate list (#3). Not yet implemented.

- iMOACO_R
  An ant-colony variant, compared against SMS-EMOA/MOEA/D/NSGA-III in
  the literature; generally competitive on DTLZ benchmark problems.
  -> New find, not previously listed. Different paradigm (ACO, not
     GA) -- similar rationale/cost profile to MOPSO/MOACO below if
     ever pursued as a "diversity of method" comparison point.

=====================================================================
4. OTHER METAHEURISTIC PARADIGMS (non-GA)
=====================================================================
- MOPSO (Multi-Objective Particle Swarm Optimization)
  Swarm-based; needs an external archive + leader-selection strategy
  for multi-objective use.
  -> Already on logs.txt's candidate list (#6). Chromosome encoding
     (gate_name, target_qubit, ctrl_qubit, angle) would need a
     continuous-relaxation or discrete-PSO adaptation -- flagged in
     logs.txt as the highest implementation cost of its list.

- MOSA (Multi-Objective Simulated Annealing)
  Pareto-dominance-based acceptance criterion + non-dominated archive,
  extending single-objective SA.
  -> Already on logs.txt's candidate list (#5, top-priority: extends
     existing sa_injection/_sa_energy machinery directly). Not yet
     implemented as a per-block optimizer.

- MOACO (Multi-Objective Ant Colony Optimization)
  Pheromone-based, often used for combinatorial/discrete problems.
  -> New find, not previously listed. Same rationale as iMOACO_R
     above -- lowest priority, no existing code to build on.

=====================================================================
5. RECENT DIRECTIONS (2025-2026)
=====================================================================
A newer survey ("From Pareto to Neural: A Mathematical Survey of
Multi-Objective Optimization Algorithms", Mathematics 2026) proposes a
five-level taxonomy -- Classical MOEA -> ML-Guided MOEA -> RL-Driven ->
LLM-Driven -> Hybrid Neuro-Evolutionary -- surveying 120+ approaches,
reflecting a shift toward learned/neural surrogate models and RL/LLM-
guided search rather than purely evolutionary operators.
This is a much bigger architectural leap than swapping selection
operators (the SMS-EMOA-style additions already in the candidate
list) -- not proposed as a near-term addition, just noted as context
for where the field is moving past this project's current scope.
