STATUS REPORT
Quantum Circuit Optimization with Genetic Algorithms (PFE)
Date: 2026-08-27

=====================================================================
OBJECTIVE
=====================================================================
Make quantum circuit optimization scale to larger circuits than
standard methods allow: split a target circuit into qubit blocks,
use a multi-objective genetic algorithm to evolve a cheaper/shallower
circuit per block that still reproduces the original's behavior with
high fidelity, then reassemble. Matters for near-term (noisy,
limited-connectivity) quantum devices, where shallower accurate
circuits are hard to get via standard, non-partitioned optimization.

Timeline: ~4 months, laptop-only, targeting a December 2026 paper
submission.

=====================================================================
WHERE WE ARE
=====================================================================
- Pipeline works end-to-end (partition -> per-block GA optimize ->
  reassemble -> re-inject lost cross-block connections -> compress),
  with infrastructure to run it reliably and repeatably: a CLI
  runner, a resumable multi-seed sweep runner, a results aggregator,
  and automated smoke tests.
- Validated on a fixed benchmark of 5 standard circuit types at 8
  qubits (260+ runs). Chosen research contribution: a second
  per-block algorithm, SMS-EMOA, compared against the original
  NSGA-II, plus mutation-operator and local-fine-tuning ablations.
  Headline finding: NSGA-II finds the single best circuit slightly
  more often; SMS-EMOA finds a wider, more diverse set of good
  trade-offs at no extra cost -- reported as complementary, not one
  replacing the other. The best supporting settings (mutation
  operator, fine-tuning) turned out to differ per algorithm rather
  than being universal.
- Performance walls that used to block anything past 8 qubits (hangs,
  1hr+ runs) have all been diagnosed and fixed. 12- and 13-qubit runs
  now complete end-to-end in minutes; a full 25-run sweep at 12 qubits
  succeeded with 0 failures. Scaling further (13-14+ qubits) still has
  one known, unsolved bottleneck (see below).
- Did a literature check of the wider multi-objective algorithm field
  to confirm SMS-EMOA was a reasonable choice and nothing better-
  suited was missed. Nothing displaces the existing plan.
