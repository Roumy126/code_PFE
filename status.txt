STATUS REPORT
Quantum Circuit Optimization with Genetic Algorithms (PFE)
Date: 2026-09-04

=====================================================================
OBJECTIVE
=====================================================================
Make quantum circuit optimization scale to larger circuits than
standard methods allow: split a target circuit into qubit blocks,
use a multi-objective genetic algorithm to evolve a cheaper/shallower
circuit per block that still reproduces the original's behavior with
high fidelity, then reassemble. Matters for near-term (noisy,
limited-connectivity) quantum devices, where shallower accurate
circuits are hard to get via standard, non-partitioned optimization.

Timeline: ~4 months, laptop-only, targeting a December 2026 paper
submission.

WHERE WE ARE

CAVEAT (2026-09-04, see item 7 below): two real bugs were just found and
fixed in the injection stage (a block-pair coverage restriction, and a
barrier-miscounting issue). Their impact is confirmed real and sometimes
large (+526% fidelity_final on one tested seed). Every fidelity_final /
algorithm-ranking / ablation finding below and in logs.txt predates this
fix and should be treated as PROVISIONAL until the re-run (in progress,
paused at 122/225 core-baseline configs) completes and is compared against
it -- do not cite these numbers in the paper without re-checking first.

- Pipeline works end-to-end (partition -> per-block GA optimize ->
  reassemble -> re-inject lost cross-block connections -> compress),
  with infrastructure to run it reliably and repeatably: a CLI
  runner, a resumable multi-seed sweep runner, a results aggregator,
  automated smoke tests, and now a report generator (generate_report.py)
  that turns results_master.csv into paper-ready tables/figures with
  no manual per-algorithm updates needed.
- Three per-block algorithms now compared head-to-head on the fixed
  8-qubit, 5-circuit, 5-seed benchmark (360+ runs across all three):
  NSGA-II (the original), SMS-EMOA, and NSGA-III (added 2026-08-28).
  Headline findings: NSGA-II finds the single best circuit slightly
  more often; SMS-EMOA finds a wider, more diverse set of good
  trade-offs at no extra cost; NSGA-III reaches close to NSGA-II's
  fidelity at roughly HALF the wall-clock cost and pairs best with
  the hybrid GA+LAS local search of the three (84% of seed-runs
  improved, vs. NSGA-II's wash and SMS-EMOA's smaller gain). Root-
  caused that speed difference the same day: it's DEAP's own default
  choice of non-dominated-sorting algorithm for NSGA-III (an
  asymptotically faster one than NSGA-II/SMS-EMOA's code both use),
  confirmed by direct isolated timing (~5x cheaper selection call,
  matching the real-run profile) -- an implementation detail of this
  codebase's current setup, not evidence NSGA-III is a fundamentally
  more efficient algorithm; report it that way, not as a general claim.
  The best supporting settings (mutation operator, fine-tuning) turned
  out to differ per algorithm rather than being universal -- now
  confirmed across three algorithms, not two, strengthening that as a
  paper finding in its own right. NSGA-III has also now been extended
  into the sa-vs-stochastic injection-method comparison (previously
  NSGA-II-only): same headline pattern (fidelity ties, injection
  method barely matters once fidelity_driven_injection's greedy pass
  dominates), but with one new nuance -- sa is cheaper than stochastic
  under NSGA-II, while stochastic is cheaper under NSGA-III, because
  NSGA-III's own block-optimization speedup shows through more when
  paired with stochastic than with sa's roughly-constant own cost.
  SMS-EMOA's sa-injection gap is now closed too (25 runs, 0 failed):
  its fidelity under sa matches its stochastic value and the same
  NSGA-II > NSGA-III > SMS-EMOA ranking already seen under stochastic,
  confirming SMS-EMOA's lower fidelity isn't an injection-method
  artifact; its sa wall-clock lands between NSGA-III's and NSGA-II's,
  unlike under stochastic where it was the slowest of the three.
  Adding a further algorithm is now a
  one-function-plus-one-registry-entry change (BLOCK_OPTIMIZERS in
  final_m1_script.py), not a multi-file edit -- built that way
  specifically so the next one (see below) is cheap to add.
- Performance walls that used to block anything past 8 qubits (hangs,
  1hr+ runs) have all been diagnosed and fixed for NSGA-II. Confirmed
  those cost benchmarks were incomplete, though: raising the sweep
  grid to each family's claimed "safe" size and actually inspecting
  results found 3 of 4 non-weak_random families were returning
  fidelity numbers at or below the estimator's noise floor (reporting
  ~0 despite completing fine), and one family (qft) didn't even
  finish (killed after 47+ min). Root-caused and fixed per family
  (different fix needed per family -- no single setting worked for
  all), validated with real reruns. Scaling fixes were validated
  under NSGA-II only so far -- not yet re-checked for NSGA-III/SMS-EMOA.
- A supervisor question ("why is NSGA-III's hypervolume lower than
  NSGA-II's?") uncovered a real measurement problem: the hypervolume
  metric was computed with a per-run adaptive reference point/
  normalization, not a shared one, so it was never actually valid to
  compare across algorithms. Fixed by adding raw Pareto-front points to
  the saved metrics and a new fair (shared fixed reference) hypervolume
  table. Honest result on the full dataset: the NSGA-II-vs-SMS-EMOA
  ranking flips under the fair metric (proof the old one was
  distorting comparisons), but NSGA-III's hypervolume gap to NSGA-II
  only shrinks (11.2% -> 6.4%), it doesn't disappear -- NSGA-III's
  front is genuinely a bit smaller even when compared fairly. Report
  fidelity_final (already directly comparable) as the primary metric
  going forward; report hypervolume only via the new fair table. Since
  extended to the mutation-scheme and hybrid-LAS ablations too (225 more
  runs, 0 failed): swap_add gives the highest fair hypervolume for all
  3 algorithms (a real coverage property, not the earlier-suspected
  normalization artifact -- but still not the same as finding more
  efficient solutions, cost_after is still worst for swap_add); fair
  hypervolume is structurally IDENTICAL between hybrid_las on/off per
  algorithm, confirmed as by-design (hybrid_las only refines the single
  best individual after the recorded front is already computed, never
  touches the population).
- Did a literature check of the wider multi-objective algorithm field
  to confirm SMS-EMOA was a reasonable choice and nothing better-
  suited was missed. Nothing displaces the existing plan.
- Answered next-research-point #2 below: NSGA-III/SMS-EMOA were scaled

  block-optimization stage running ~5-9% cheaper than NSGA-II's -- real
