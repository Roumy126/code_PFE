=====================================================================
PROJECT OVERVIEW
=====================================================================
Academic research project (French PFE — end-of-studies project) on
optimizing quantum circuits with genetic algorithms. Goal: take a
target quantum circuit (or unitary), partition it, and evolve a
cheaper/shallower circuit that still reproduces the target's behavior
with high fidelity.

Stack: Qiskit (circuit simulation) + DEAP (evolutionary computation,
NSGA-II / NSGA-III style multi-objective optimization).

The codebase is a research sandbox, not a packaged library: a
progression of standalone Jupyter notebooks and scripts, each an
iteration of the same core pipeline (mono-objective -> multi-objective
-> block-decomposed/scalable). No shared package, no test suite, no
build step.

=====================================================================
REPOSITORY STRUCTURE (reading order / sophistication progression)
=====================================================================
1. AG_mono/ — mono-objective GA: evolve a chromosome (gate sequence)
   approximating a target unitary, optimizing fidelity only.
2. NSGA-II/, NSGA-III/ — multi-objective versions (fidelity, depth,
   gate cost) using DEAP's NSGA-II/NSGA-III selection. NSGA-III/
   AG_multi_objectifs_NSGA3.ipynb exists only on this branch
   (ULBS_qiea) — see "OPEN ITEM — RESOLVED" for its review status.
3. m1/, m1_bloc_indep/, M1_indicateurs_version/, M1_finale/ — "M1"
   line: adds circuit partitioning into qubit blocks (NSGA-II runs
   per-block) plus quality indicators (hypervolume, IGD, spread,
   epsilon). M1_finale/ is the most complete version.
   -> M1_finale/final_m1_script.py is the canonical, most-maintained
      pipeline implementation.
4. m2_bloc_chevauche/ — "block overlap" variant: blocks may share
   qubits at interfaces rather than being a strict partition.
5. Final_test/, final_test_AG/, test/ — later validation notebooks
   applying the pipeline to specific circuits (QAOA, MaxCut, W-state,
   QFT, VQE, 14/64-qubit cases). Throwaway experiment copies, not a
   distinct architecture.

Duplicated notebook snapshots (e.g. "code_travaille copy 3.ipynb")
are not divergent components — don't assume fixes need to be
propagated across copies without checking first.

=====================================================================
CORE PIPELINE (M1_finale/final_m1_script.py) — reference implementation
=====================================================================
Entry point: optimise_circuit_pipeline(...)

1. Partitioning — build_interaction_graph (qubit-interaction graph
   weighted by 2-qubit gate count), louvain_partition (Louvain
   community detection into blocks; NOTE: no seed/random_state of its
   own, see "SCALING" below), multilevel_partition (Metis/KL
   alternative), extract_interblock_gates (captures cross-block gates
   for later reinjection).
2. Sub-circuit extraction / recomposition — extract_subcircuit /
   recompose_from_blocks stitch optimized block circuits back into a
   global circuit in original gate order.
3. Fidelity metric — compute_fidelity: exact operator-overlap fidelity
   |Tr(U_circ . U_target^dagger)| / 2^n, short-circuits to 1.0 above 15
   qubits (dense-matrix blowup). safe_fidelity_between_circuits is the
   scale-aware version used everywhere else in the pipeline — see
   "SCALING" for its exact/approximate tiers and history.
4. Intra-block optimization — optimise_block_nsga2 (DEAP NSGA-II,
   3 objectives: maximize fidelity, minimize depth, minimize
   chromosome-length cost) and optimise_block_smsemoa (hypervolume-
   driven alternative, see "RESEARCH ANGLE CHOSEN AND IMPLEMENTED").
   update_rotation_angles ("LAS") is an optional local angle-search
   refinement after the GA (hybrid_las param).
5. Inter-block gate injection (re-adds cross-block entanglement lost
   during block-local optimization) — sa_injection (simulated
   annealing), stochastic_injection (random trial-and-keep), and
   fidelity_driven_injection (greedy complement pass that ALWAYS runs
   afterward regardless of injection_method, and per the 8-qubit
   baseline usually produces the final circuit).
6. Compression — hand-rolled passes (cancel_inverse_gates,
   merge_rotations, remove_negligible_rotations, composed in
   compress_custom) plus a Qiskit PassManager pass (qiskit_opt_pass).
7. Multi-objective quality indicators — evaluate_run / compute_hv /
   spread_delta / compute_spacing / compute_epsilon (hypervolume,
   spread, spacing, epsilon, IGD when a reference front is supplied),
   tracked per generation. Figures written to out_figs/.

Later stages depend on earlier ones' output shape — check
optimise_circuit_pipeline's call sequence before changing an output
type. random_weakly_connected_circuit and the 4 benchmark generators
(qaoa_maxcut_circuit, w_state_circuit, qft_circuit,
hw_efficient_ansatz_circuit) generate the project's test circuits.

SCALING
=====================================================================
Status as of 2026-08-27: the pipeline's fidelity computation has two
tiers — exact (dense Operator trace, n <= fidelity_exact_threshold,
default 13) and approximate (Monte-Carlo estimate above it). Both tiers
had genuine performance bugs; all found-and-fixed so far were redundant
computation, not new algorithms, and were verified either byte-identical
or algebraically exact before adoption.

Timeline of what was found and fixed:
- 2026-08-25: research scope pivoted to a fixed 8-qubit benchmark set
  after 12/16/20-qubit attempts repeatedly hung/took hours (deferred
  rather than debugged blind).
- 2026-08-26: profiled instead of guessing, and found the injection
  stage's three main strategies were each independently rebuilding and
  re-simulating whole circuits on every one of hundreds of trials, when
  each had a cheap closed-form or cached-operator shortcut available:
  sa_injection's energy check compares a candidate against its own
  unchanged base, so the base cancels out algebraically (~1000x+
  speedup, exact); fidelity_driven_injection caches the candidate/target
  operator product once and updates it incrementally per trial via a
  partial-trace identity instead of rebuilding from scratch (~40x+,
  exact, verified bit-identical); stochastic_injection's one-gate-diff
  fidelity reduces to a 4x4 local trace with zero dependence on circuit
  size (~900x+, exact). Combined effect: real qaoa_maxcut pilot sweeps
  at n=12/13 (previously infeasible) completed in under 10 minutes, and
  n=12 became practical at full production GA settings across all 5
  benchmark families (~80-240s/run).
- 2026-08-27: cached the pipeline's 5 reporting-level fidelity checks'
  shared target-circuit operator, which was being rebuilt from scratch
  on every call despite the target never changing (~1.4x faster at
  n=12/13, verified byte-identical).
- 2026-08-27: replaced the approximate tier's SWAP-test estimator with
  an algebraically equivalent but much cheaper "fidelity-echo" circuit
  (same target quantity |<psi|U^dagger V|psi>|^2, computed on n qubits
  instead of 2n+1, with no CSWAP-induced entanglement between two
  coupled registers) — the SWAP test's own construction, not entanglement
  in the target circuits per se, was what made it exponentially
  expensive for genuinely entangled families (QAOA/QFT/W-state/hw-
  efficient-ansatz). Verified correct against exact values and the old
  estimator; a case that used to hang 5+ minutes per call now takes
  ~0.06s. Renamed accordingly (fidelity_backend: "swap_test_mc" ->
  "echo_test_mc"; CLI flags --fidelity-swap-* -> --fidelity-echo-*).
- 2026-08-27: mapped the new backend's actual per-family reach and found
  qaoa_maxcut is the one family that's still genuinely hard past ~n=16
  (its graph structure really does get more entangled as n grows — a
  physical limit, not a software artifact). The other 4 families
  (weak_random, w_state, hw_efficient_ansatz, qft) now scale comfortably
  to n=20-32+. Root-caused why QAOA still stalled at n=16 even with the
  fix: fidelity_driven_injection's max_trials was hardcoded at 300 (the
  one stage-iteration-count never exposed as a tunable parameter) with
  no incremental-cache fast path above the approximate-tier threshold.
  Exposed it as --fidelity-driven-max-trials (default unchanged); a
  previously-stuck n=16 QAOA run completed in ~200s once it and the
  reporting-level sample/shot counts were turned down.
- 2026-08-27: gave fidelity_driven_injection's per-trial loop its own
  fast path above the exact tier (fidelity_exact_threshold <
  n <= new statevector_fast_path_threshold, default 24), instead of
  resimulating the whole candidate circuit via the MPS-backed fidelity-
  echo estimator on every trial. Per trial, the loop only ever needs
  <chi|G_trial^dagger|phi> for phi = target_qc|psi>, chi = candidate_qc|psi>
  (a handful of fixed random product states |psi>) -- exactly the
  quantity the approximate branch estimates, just computed EXACTLY (no
  shot noise) via two new helpers, _statevector_pair_reduce and
  _apply_local_gate_to_statevector, that reduce it to an O(2^n) partial
  trace on cached state vectors (phi fixed for the whole call; chi
  updated incrementally on acceptance, not resimulated). Verified
  correct three ways: against brute-force outer-product + the existing
  _partial_trace_reduce; against direct re-simulation of each trial's
  test_qc (exact match, since both are noise-free); and against the old
  MPS/shot-based estimator at high shot count (converges, as expected).
  Real-run result: the n=16 qaoa_maxcut case above -- previously stuck
  even at reduced --fidelity-driven-max-trials/sample counts -- now runs
  the FULL default 300 trials in ~21s of injection-stage time (down from
  ~175s at 20 trials before this fix); with reporting-call cost also
  isolated, total wall clock dropped from "didn't finish in 280s" to
  ~50s. New CLI flag: --fidelity-driven-statevector-threshold. New
  meta field: fidelity_driven_tier ("exact"/"statevector"/"echo_test_mc").
  Did NOT touch the 5 pipeline-level reporting calls (fid_rebuilt/
  fid_rebuilt1/fid_inj/fid_i/fid_final) at the time -- those still routed
  through the MPS-based estimator above fidelity_exact_threshold, which
  is why a real n=20 qaoa_maxcut run still timed out (fixed next).
- 2026-08-27: gave those 5 reporting calls the same exact-statevector
  option, but as an EXPLICIT opt-in rather than a default switch --
  benchmarked first, and confirmed MPS and statevector have OPPOSITE
  strengths: statevector is ~150x+ faster than MPS for qaoa_maxcut at
  n=16 (its entanglement is what MPS chokes on), but ~250-400x SLOWER
  than MPS for w_state/hw_efficient_ansatz at n=24 (their low
  entanglement is exactly what MPS exploits and statevector cannot).
  Blindly switching backends would have fixed QAOA while badly
  regressing the 4 families already scaling well. New
  approximate_gate_fidelity_statevector_mc (same target quantity, exact
  per sample, no shot noise) plus a new safe_fidelity_between_circuits
  param approximate_backend ("mps" default, unchanged behavior
  everywhere existing callers don't opt in; "statevector" new). Exposed
  on the pipeline as fidelity_approximate_backend and
  --fidelity-approximate-backend on the CLI -- chosen per circuit family
  the same way injection_method/block_algorithm/mutation_scheme already
  are, no automatic detection. Verified against exact ground truth
  (bit-identical) and against the old echo-test estimator at very high
  shot count (converges). Real result: the n=20 qaoa_maxcut case that
  previously timed out even with reduced injection settings now
  completes in 37.8s with --fidelity-approximate-backend statevector.

- 2026-08-28: raised run_sweep.py's CIRCUIT_QUBIT_SIZES grid off its flat
  8/12-qubit baseline via a real pilot (1 seed per family at its

Root cause of nsga3's speed in (a) -- RESOLVED (2026-08-28 follow-up):
Two independent measurements agree. (1) cProfile on a real block-optimizer
call (matched seed, same 3-qubit block extracted from a real weak_random
circuit, generations=100/pop_size=100 for all three): selNSGA2's cumulative
time was 2.27-2.40s over 100-101 calls; SMS-EMOA's tools.sortNondominated
(called TWICE per generation -- once in rank_by_front for mating-pool
Pareto rank, once in environmental_select) totaled 2.82s over 201 calls;
selNSGA3's cumulative time was only 0.07-0.19s over 100 calls. (2) A
cProfile-free isolated timing test (tools.selNSGA2 / tools.selNSGA3 /
tools.sortNondominated called directly, 100 trials, on matched synthetic
200-individual populations with random 3-objective fitness values, so no
confound from fitness evaluation or profiler overhead): selNSGA2 11.40
ms/call (1.14s/100 gens), selNSGA3 2.16 ms/call (0.22s/100 gens, ~5.2x
cheaper), sortNondominated 11.62 ms/call so SMS-EMOA's 2x/generation usage
costs 23.20 ms/gen (2.32s/100 gens, ~10.5x nsga3's per-generation cost).
Cause: DEAP's selNSGA3 defaults to nd="log" -- Fortin & Buzdalov's
asymptotically faster non-dominated sort (O(N.log^(M-1)N),
tools.sortLogNondominated), while selNSGA2 and this codebase's hand-rolled
SMS-EMOA (rank_by_front + environmental_select) both call the classic
O(M.N^2) tools.sortNondominated -- SMS-EMOA pays that cost twice per
generation, which is why it isn't the cheapest of the three despite nsga3
and smsemoa sharing similar total wall-clock in (a) (smsemoa's other
per-generation work, e.g. hv_trim_boundary_front, must be correspondingly
lighter to end up close to nsga2's total).
IMPORTANT CAVEAT for how to report this: this is a DEAP default-parameter/
implementation difference, NOT evidence that NSGA-III's algorithm is
inherently more efficient than NSGA-II or SMS-EMOA as MOO algorithms.
tools.sortLogNondominated is a standalone function DEAP exposes -- nothing
stops rank_by_front/environmental_select (or a modified selNSGA2 substitute)
from calling it instead of the classic sort, which would likely close most
of this gap. Report "nsga3 is ~2x cheaper in this codebase's current
implementation, due to its default non-dominated-sort algorithm" -- not
"NSGA-III is a faster algorithm than NSGA-II/SMS-EMOA" as a general claim.
Not investigated: how much of the remaining wall-clock gap (the isolated
selection-only test explains roughly 0.9-2.1s of the ~4-5s real-run gap)
comes from other main-process-only overhead (assignCrowdingDist for nsga2,

HYPERVOLUME COMPARABILITY ACROSS ALGORITHMS (2026-08-28)
=====================================================================
Supervisor question, prompted by the algorithm_hypervolume.png figure:
why is nsga3's mean_hv (0.0462) lower than nsga2's (0.0520) -- shouldn't
higher hypervolume mean a better Pareto front? Correct in general, but
NOT valid to conclude from this codebase's mean_hv as computed, and this
needed checking properly rather than answered from intuition alone.

Root cause: evaluate_run (final_m1_script.py) computes HV with a PER-RUN
ADAPTIVE reference point AND normalization -- f_min/f_max come from that
run's own final population (normalize_objectives(costs)), and the
reference point defaults to that run's own normalized front's worst point
plus a fixed margin (ref = np.max(P_eval, axis=0) + 0.1). Every run
effectively gets its own private [0,1]-ish coordinate system and its own
reference point. This is fine for tracking ONE run's convergence across
generations (plot_moo_history's use case, and what this code was written
for) but invalid for ranking DIFFERENT algorithms against each other --
the same absolute hypervolume number means something different depending
on how that particular run's own population happened to spread out. This
is the same underlying mechanism already flagged once before, for a
different pair, in "RESEARCH ANGLE CHOSEN AND IMPLEMENTED" above:
"swap_add's outlier mean_hv ... is a per-run-normalization/bloat artifact,
not a real front-quality win."

Fix: added a "front_raw" field to evaluate_run's returned dict -- the
Pareto front's (fidelity, depth, cost) in natural, unnormalized units
(F[idx], i.e. literally the original per-individual fitness values of the
non-dominated set) -- purely additive, does not change HV/Spread/Spacing/
ref_point's existing (adaptive, in-loop) behavior at all. This flows
through automatically into metrics.json's moo_metrics_per_block (no other
pipeline change needed) since that's already just whatever evaluate_run's
last-generation call returns. Verified via pytest (15/15 still pass) and a
real run's metrics.json (front_raw present, correct natural-unit values).

Added generate_report.py's table_fair_hv_comparison: for each (circuit,
seed) with front_raw from >=2 algorithms, pools all present algorithms'
front points PER BLOCK INDEX (blocks are identical across algorithms for
the same circuit+seed -- partitioning happens before block_algorithm is
chosen), derives ONE shared min/max normalization from the pooled points,
and evaluates each algorithm's HV against ONE shared FIXED reference point
([1.1, 1.1, 1.1] in normalized space -- same "+0.1 margin past the worst
point" convention as the adaptive version, just made shared rather than
per-run). Required regenerating metrics.json for the 75-run algorithm-
comparison baseline (nsga2/smsemoa/nsga3 x 5 circuits x 5 seeds) so those
files would carry front_raw -- the existing 75 were moved to
runs_backup_pre_front_raw/ (not deleted; a bulk rm -rf was blocked by the
permission classifier, and moving aside was the safer choice anyway) and
re-run via run_sweep.py, 0 failed, deterministic (same seeds -> same
optimization outcome, only the metrics.json schema gained a field).

Result, on the full 25-seed comparison (not just a single matched-block
spot-check, which was tried first and turned out to be too small a sample
to trust -- see below):
  block_algorithm  mean_hv (adaptive)  fair_mean_hv (shared reference)
  nsga2                        0.0520                           0.8149
  nsga3                        0.0462                           0.7630
  smsemoa                      0.0596                           0.8081
Two distinct things happen under the fair metric, and both matter:
1. nsga2-vs-smsemoa RANKING FLIPS: smsemoa looks best under the adaptive
   metric (0.0596 > 0.0520), but nsga2 is marginally ahead under the fair
   one (0.8149 > 0.8081, an ~0.8% margin -- essentially a tie, but the
   DIRECTION is reversed). Direct proof the adaptive metric distorts
   cross-algorithm comparisons for a pair that was never even in question.
2. nsga2-vs-nsga3 GAP SHRINKS BUT DOES NOT DISAPPEAR: adaptive relative

Hybrid-LAS ablation, fair hypervolume: IDENTICAL between hybrid_las=False
and hybrid_las=True for every algorithm (nsga2 0.8149/0.8149, nsga3
0.7630/0.7630, smsemoa 0.8081/0.8081 -- to displayed precision). Verified
this is a genuine structural property, not a bug: in all three
optimise_block_* functions, final_metrics = evaluate_run(...) (the call
that produces front_raw, hence any HV computed from it) runs on `pop`
BEFORE hybrid_las is applied; hybrid_las only refines a COPY of the single
best_ind afterward and never touches `pop` or triggers another
evaluate_run call. So hybrid_las structurally cannot affect the recorded
per-block Pareto-front metrics (HV, Spread, Spacing, front_raw, adaptive
or fair) -- its entire effect is on fidelity_final (via the final built
circuit), which IS where the existing hybrid_las_ablation table already
shows its effect. This wasn't previously visible because
table_hybrid_las_ablation never surfaced mean_hv at all -- worth keeping
in mind when reading any hypervolume-based comparison of hybrid_las
runs: there is nothing to compare, by construction, at the per-block level.
